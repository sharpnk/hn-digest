## 5. [Black hole singularity is a surface not a point](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49437210) (↑145, 💬101)
**Article:** A paper by Hamilton and McMaken argues the common pop-science claim that a black hole's singularity is a point is false — two infalling observers on different angular trajectories lose causal contact before reaching it, making the singularity a surface, not a point. For rotating black holes, the singular surface likely resides at the inner horizon, where perturbations trigger mass inflation instability. The authors suggest quantum gravity states may reside at this effectively 2-dimensional singular surface.

**Comments:** Several commenters point out this is not a new research result but a correction of popular misconceptions, noting any GR grad student or a Penrose diagram would show the same. There is debate over whether the singularity's topology is even well-defined, and discussion of related concepts like fuzzballs, frame dragging, and the inner horizon. Others share explanatory resources and analogies for laypeople, and some branch into off-topic musings about "reasoning black holes" and cosmology.
