## 20. [Differences Between `Foldl` and `Foldr`](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49917900) (↑119, 💬27)
**Article:** The Haskell blog reproduces Alexis King’s explanation that foldl and foldr both traverse lists left-to-right but differ in associativity: foldl groups applications to the left, while foldr groups them to the right. In strict languages, foldl can be tail-recursive in constant space, whereas foldr needs stack proportional to the list because it effectively reduces from the end. In lazy Haskell, foldl builds thunks and should usually be replaced by foldl', while foldr can be productive with lazy operations but still needs care with strict ones.

**Comments:** Commenters note that foldr passes control flow to the accumulating function, enabling early termination and arbitrary traversals, and some prefer for_ or foldMap/monoids for clarity. A debate arises over whether saying both folds traverse left-to-right is misleading for non-linked structures, where foldr on arrays or doubly linked lists may traverse right-to-left. Others discuss AI overuse of folds, explicit recursion versus folds, and when folds genuinely improve readability.
