## 30. [Io_uring Without Readahead](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49521623) (↑101, 💬32)
**Article:** This technical post examines Turso's io_uring backend, which uses O_DIRECT and thus loses kernel readahead, forcing a single in-flight read at a time. Adding application-level readahead of 32 pages dramatically improves performance by allowing the block layer to merge contiguous requests into far fewer, larger I/Os. The author also analyzes the costs of SQ polling threads and finds that O_DIRECT incurs more CPU cache misses than buffered I/O because the kernel's copy step naturally warms the caches.

**Comments:** Commenters debate the benchmarking approach, noting that embedded databases don't own their hardware and shouldn't assume uncontended resources—a common pitfall when taking cache control away from the kernel. Several suggest alternatives like preadv or preadv2 with RWF_NOWAIT as cheaper ways to achieve the same readahead batching, and there's discussion of whether one large read beats many parallel small reads on modern NVMe. Others share personal experiments, with one claiming mmap combined with io_uring send operations was the fastest way to serve file contents to clients.
