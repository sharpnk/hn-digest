## 10. [Why is the x86 undefined instruction called ud2? Why 2?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49683262) (↑165, 💬39)
**Article:** Raymond Chen explains that x86's ud2 is an architecturally guaranteed invalid-opcode instruction used by compilers to mark unreachable code, and traces its name to the retroactive naming of earlier accidental invalid opcodes 0F FF as ud0 and 0F B9 as ud1. It notes ud2's advantage as a parameterless two-byte instruction with consistent behavior, unlike ud0/ud1 decoding quirks.

**Comments:** Commenters discuss whether assembly knowledge is still needed, alternatives like INT3 or software interrupts, and how ud2 compares to INT3 for crashing or debugging. The thread also adds history and technical details about UD0/UD1/UD2, INT vs invalid-opcode handling, and a side discussion on why hard drives start at C:.
